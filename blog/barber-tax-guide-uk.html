Tax Guide for Barbers: Chair Rental, Cash, Tips and Expenses

By Sardar Muhammad, AAT • Updated 13 October 2026 • 4 min read

Quick Summary

  • Most chair-renting barbers are self-employed and must file a Self-Assessment return.
  • All takings count as income — cash, card and tips.
  • Chair rent, clippers, products, insurance and training are allowable expenses.
  • Barbershop owners must include chair rental income and watch the £90,000 VAT threshold.
  • HMRC sees card machine data, so records must match what goes through the till.

Barbering is a busy, cash-heavy trade, and HMRC knows it. Whether you rent a chair, work as a self-employed barber in someone else's shop, or own your own barbershop, getting your tax right protects you from bills, penalties and enquiries later. This guide covers what barbers in Glasgow and across the UK need to know.

Employed or self-employed?

Your tax position depends on how you actually work, not just what you call yourself:

  • Self-employed (chair renter): you pay a fixed rent for a chair, set your own hours and prices, keep your own takings and bring your own tools.
  • Employed: the shop owner sets your hours, takes the takings and pays you a wage or commission through payroll.

If a shop calls you self-employed but controls your hours, prices and clients, HMRC may treat you as an employee. Shop owners should be clear about this, because they can become liable for unpaid PAYE and National Insurance.

What counts as income?

Everything you take from clients counts as income:

  • Cash payments — record them daily
  • Card payments through your own card reader (Square, SumUp, Zettle and others)
  • Tips — cash and card tips are taxable
  • Product sales — wax, oils and other retail items

Payment providers and online booking platforms share data with HMRC, and digital platforms now report sellers' income automatically. HMRC compares these figures with what's on your tax return, so under-declaring card takings is easy to spot — and cash should be recorded with the same care.

Phone and social media advertisingBusiness proportion
Laundry and work clothingBranded tunics or protective clothing, not everyday clothes
Travel to other locationsMobile barbers: 45p a mile for the first 10,000 business miles

Ordinary clothes and your own haircuts are not allowable, even if you wear them at work.

Owning a barbershop

As a shop owner, you have extra things to manage:

  • Chair rental income from self-employed barbers is part of your business income.
  • Employees need payroll, minimum wage, auto-enrolment pensions and, from October 2024, a fair tips policy — all tips must be passed on to workers in full.
  • Premises costs — rent, business rates, utilities, cleaning and equipment. Many small shops in Scotland qualify for Small Business Bonus Scheme rates relief.

VAT for barbershops

The VAT registration threshold is £90,000 of turnover over any rolling 12 months. Busy shops can reach it quickly, especially when all takings go through one business. Once registered, you charge 20% VAT on haircuts. The Flat Rate Scheme rate for hairdressing is 13%, which can be simpler for some shops. Read our VAT threshold guide before you get close.

Making Tax Digital

From 6 April 2026, self-employed people with turnover (not profit) over £50,000 must keep digital records and send quarterly updates to HMRC using compatible software. The threshold drops to £30,000 from April 2027 and is planned to drop to £20,000 from April 2028. See our Making Tax Digital guide.

Simple record-keeping tips

  • Use a separate bank account for the business
  • Record cash takings at the end of every day
  • Photograph receipts as soon as you get them
  • Set aside around 20–25% of profits for tax and National Insurance

Frequently Asked Questions

Do self-employed barbers pay tax on tips?

Yes. Tips are part of your taxable income whether they are paid in cash or by card. Record them with the rest of your takings.

Can I claim chair rent as an expense?

Yes. If you're a self-employed barber renting a chair, the rent you pay is a fully allowable business expense.

Do barbers need to register for VAT?

Only if taxable turnover goes over £90,000 in a rolling 12-month period. Individual chair renters rarely reach this, but barbershop owners often do.

Can HMRC see my card machine payments?

HMRC can obtain data from card payment providers and digital platforms and uses it to check that income declared on tax returns is complete.

What records should a barber keep?

Daily takings (cash, card and tips), receipts for expenses, bank statements and chair rental agreements. Keep records for at least 5 years after the 31 January filing deadline.
