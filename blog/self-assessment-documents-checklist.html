Self-Assessment Checklist: Documents You Need for Your Tax Return

By Sardar Muhammad, AAT • Updated 13 October 2026 • 4 min read

Quick Summary

  • Always have your UTR, National Insurance number and HMRC login details ready.
  • Employees need their P60, any P45 and P11D.
  • Self-employed people and landlords need income and expense records for 6 April to 5 April.
  • Don't forget savings interest, dividends, pension contributions and Gift Aid.
  • Starting early gives time to find missing paperwork before 31 January.

Most of the stress of a tax return comes from hunting for paperwork. Use this checklist to gather everything for the 2025/26 tax year (6 April 2025 to 5 April 2026) — the return is due online by 31 January 2027. Tick off the sections that apply to you.

1. Your details

  • Unique Taxpayer Reference (UTR)
  • National Insurance number
  • HMRC online account login
  • Bank details for any refund
  • Any letters from HMRC during the year, such as tax code notices

2. Employment income

  • P60 from each employer you worked for on 5 April
  • P45 from any job you left during the year
  • P11D or payrolled benefits details (company car, health insurance)
  • Receipts for work expenses your employer didn't repay
  • Details of tips or other income not taxed through payroll

3. Self-employment

  • Sales invoices or a record of all takings (cash, card and online)
  • Business bank statements for the full year
  • Receipts for expenses — see our allowable expenses list
  • Mileage log
  • Details of equipment or vehicles bought or sold
  • CIS payment and deduction statements, if you're a subcontractor

4. Property income

  • Rent received for each property (letting agent statements)
  • Mortgage interest statements
  • Receipts for repairs, insurance, agent fees, landlord registration and other costs
  • Ownership share if the property is jointly owned

5. Savings and investments

  • Bank and building society interest statements (not ISAs)
  • Dividend statements from shares and your own company
  • Details of any shares, crypto or property sold — dates, costs and proceeds
  • Foreign income or gains

6. Reliefs and other items

  • Personal pension contributions (not workplace pensions taken from your pay)
  • Gift Aid donations
  • Student loan plan type
  • Child Benefit received, if either partner's income is over £60,000
  • Marriage Allowance transfer, if applicable
  • Payments on account you've already made

Tips for a smooth return

  • Start in the autumn — missing documents take time to replace.
  • Check figures against bank statements rather than memory.
  • Keep everything for at least 5 years after the filing deadline (22 months for those not in business or letting property).

Sending this to an accountant? A photo or scan of each item is usually all we need.

Frequently Asked Questions

What documents do I need for Self-Assessment?

Your UTR and National Insurance number, plus records for each income source: P60, P45 and P11D for employment, income and expense records for self-employment or property, and statements for savings, dividends, pension contributions and Gift Aid.

Do I need my P60 for Self-Assessment?

Yes, if you were employed at the end of the tax year. It shows your pay and the tax deducted, which you enter on your return.

How long should I keep tax records?

At least 5 years after the 31 January deadline if you're self-employed or a landlord, and at least 22 months after the end of the tax year otherwise.

Do I need to include ISA interest on my tax return?

No. Interest and gains inside an ISA are tax-free and don't go on your return.

When is the 2025/26 Self-Assessment deadline?

Online returns and payment for the 2025/26 tax year are due by 31 January 2027. Paper returns are due by 31 October 2026.
