Auto-Enrolment Pensions: A Step-by-Step Guide for Small Employers

By Sardar Muhammad, AAT • Updated 13 October 2026 • 4 min read

Quick Summary

  • Your duties start on the day your first employee starts work.
  • Enrol staff aged 22 to State Pension age earning over £10,000 a year (£833 a month).
  • Minimum contribution is 8% of qualifying earnings (£6,240 to £50,270), with the employer paying at least 3%.
  • File your declaration of compliance with The Pensions Regulator within 5 months of your duties start date.
  • Re-enrol eligible staff who opted out every 3 years.

Every UK employer must offer a workplace pension and automatically enrol eligible staff — even if you only employ one person part-time. The rules are run by The Pensions Regulator (TPR), and missing a step can lead to fines. Here is how auto-enrolment works for small employers in 2026/27.

When do your duties start?

Your duties start date is the day your first member of staff begins work. From that date you must assess everyone you employ, every time you run payroll. A limited company where the only workers are directors without employment contracts is usually exempt — but tell TPR so they don't chase you.

Step 1: Assess your staff each pay period

Worker typeAge and earnings (2026/27)What you must do
Eligible jobholder22 to State Pension age, earning over £10,000 a yearAutomatically enrol and contribute
Non-eligible jobholder16–74 earning £6,240–£10,000, or aged 16–21 / State Pension age–74 earning over £10,000Enrol if they ask, and contribute
Entitled worker16–74 earning £6,240 or lessLet them join if they ask; employer contribution optional

The thresholds are applied per pay period, so a weekly-paid worker is assessed against £192 a week, not the annual figure. Good payroll software does this automatically.

Step 2: Choose a qualifying pension scheme

You must use a scheme that meets the auto-enrolment rules. Many small employers use NEST (the government-backed scheme, which must accept any employer), The People's Pension or Smart Pension. Check fees and how easily the scheme connects to your payroll software.

Step 3: Enrol staff and write to them

Enrol eligible jobholders within six weeks of their start date (or of becoming eligible), and write to every member of staff explaining how auto-enrolment applies to them. You can use postponement to delay assessment for up to three months — for example during a probation period — but you must write to the employee to tell them.

Step 4: Pay the minimum contributions

Contributions are calculated on qualifying earnings — gross pay between £6,240 and £50,270 a year:

  • Employer: at least 3%
  • Employee: the rest, normally 5% (including tax relief)
  • Total minimum: 8%

Example

An employee earns £24,000 a year. Qualifying earnings are £24,000 − £6,240 = £17,760. The employer pays at least 3% (£532.80 a year) and the employee 5% (£888 a year).

Contributions must reach the pension scheme by the 22nd of the month after they are deducted (19th if paying by cheque).

Step 5: Declaration of compliance

Within 5 months of your duties start date, you must complete a declaration of compliance on TPR's website. It confirms you have met your duties. This is the step small employers most often miss — and it is the one TPR fines first.

Ongoing duties

  • Every pay run: assess new and existing staff.
  • Opt-outs: staff can opt out within one month of being enrolled and get a full refund. You must not encourage them to opt out.
  • Re-enrolment: every 3 years, re-enrol eligible staff who left the scheme and file a re-declaration.
  • Records: keep auto-enrolment records for 6 years (opt-out notices for 4 years).

Penalties for getting it wrong

TPR can issue a £400 fixed penalty, followed by escalating daily penalties from £50 to £10,000 a day depending on how many staff you employ. Penalties are avoidable with a reliable payroll process.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need to auto-enrol if I only have one employee?

Yes. Auto-enrolment applies as soon as you employ your first member of staff. Companies where the only workers are directors without employment contracts are usually exempt.

What is the auto-enrolment earnings trigger for 2026/27?

The earnings trigger is £10,000 a year (£833 a month or £192 a week). Staff aged 22 to State Pension age earning above it must be automatically enrolled.

How much must employers contribute to a workplace pension?

At least 3% of qualifying earnings, which are earnings between £6,240 and £50,270 a year. Total contributions from employer and employee must be at least 8%.

What is a declaration of compliance?

It is a declaration to The Pensions Regulator that you have met your auto-enrolment duties. It must be completed within 5 months of your duties start date, and again after each 3-yearly re-enrolment.

What happens if I don't comply with auto-enrolment?

The Pensions Regulator can issue a £400 fixed penalty and then escalating daily penalties of £50 to £10,000 a day, depending on the number of staff you employ.
