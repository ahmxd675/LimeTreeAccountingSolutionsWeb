UK Landlords with International Tenants: Rent, Records and Tax

By Sardar Muhammad, AAT • 28 September 2026 • 6 min read

Keep the tax position separate from the payment route

A tenant may be studying, working or living outside the UK, and rent may arrive from an overseas bank account. For a UK property, those details do not by themselves change the basic need to keep records of the rental income and report it correctly. The landlord's own tax residence, ownership and wider circumstances can affect the return, so avoid assuming every arrangement is treated identically.

Record each payment clearly

Keep the tenancy agreement, rent schedule, bank or payment-platform statements and a note of any fees or deductions. Where a payment arrives in a foreign currency, retain the original amount, date received, currency-conversion evidence and the sterling figure used in your accounts. Apply a consistent, supportable conversion method and keep enough detail to explain it later.

If a payment is short, late or combined with another transfer, reconcile it against the rent due rather than relying on the bank statement alone. Record deposits separately from rent and keep evidence of any amounts returned or applied under the tenancy.

Does a tenant living abroad change UK rental tax?

The tenant's nationality or overseas address is not a substitute for working out the landlord's tax position. Income from letting UK property is generally considered under UK property-income rules. If you are also non-resident, the Non-Resident Landlord Scheme may apply; see our guide to UK rental income when you live abroad.

Build a useful property file

  • Keep a separate income and expense schedule for each property.
  • Match rent received to the agreement and the period it covers.
  • Record bank charges, transfer fees and any deductions separately.
  • Keep invoices and evidence for repairs, insurance and letting costs.
  • Store tenancy, ownership and deposit records with the relevant tax-year documents.

Tenancy, immigration, identity-check and deposit-protection rules are separate from tax record keeping. Check the applicable housing requirements or seek specialist advice; an accounting record cannot replace those checks.

When to get help

Ask an accountant if you receive rent in another currency, own property jointly, live outside the UK, have several properties or are unsure how a payment should be reported. A well-organised schedule makes Self Assessment easier and helps explain the figures if HMRC asks questions.

Frequently asked questions

Can my tenant pay rent from an overseas account?

Payment logistics depend on your tenancy arrangements and bank. Keep clear evidence of the amount, date, currency and any charges so the payment can be matched to rent due.

Should I record the rent in the tenant's currency?

Keep the original transaction details and the sterling amount used for your UK records, along with the conversion method and evidence.

Does an overseas tenant mean I need a different tax return?

Not solely because the tenant lives abroad. Your own residence, ownership and income determine what you need to report. Take advice if you are not UK-resident or have cross-border income.
